Research

Exploring robust visual perception through research and implementation.

My research explores feature correspondence, geometric alignment, and efficient visual perception. I enjoy turning research ideas into reproducible implementations and evaluating them through systematic experiments.

I am particularly interested in areas that connect computer vision with robotics, autonomous systems, and computer graphics, such as inverse rendering, 4D vision, and video understanding.

Featured Research

Journal Manuscript · Under Review

A Clifford-Structured Geometric Transformer for Rotation-Aware Geometric Alignment

A grade-aware dense correspondence framework that separates scalar, vector, and rotor-related cues to study geometric alignment under severe in-plane rotation.

Problem
Dense geometric alignment under severe in-plane rotation and ambiguous visual evidence.
Approach
  • Clifford Algebra inspired representation supports dense correspondence estimation.
  • The framework combines scalar, vector, and bivector cues in a geometric transformer.
  • Direct residual correction and energy-based refinement improve geometric consistency.
My Role
Conceptualization, methodology, software, validation, formal analysis, investigation, data curation, visualization, and original manuscript drafting.

Research Approach

I approach research by connecting a clearly defined problem with reproducible implementation and evaluation. I begin by identifying limitations in existing methods, develop and test a proposed approach, and analyze where the method succeeds or fails.

Through this process, I have gained experience in experimental design, model implementation, quantitative evaluation, visualization, and academic writing.

Problem Formulation

Define what fails, why it matters, and how improvement should be measured.

Implementation

Translate research ideas into reproducible experiments and inspectable code.

Evaluation

Compare behavior through metrics, visualizations, and failure-case analysis.

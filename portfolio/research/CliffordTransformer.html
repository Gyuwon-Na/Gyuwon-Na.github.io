01

Research Overview

What this research investigates

Dense correspondence models are usually trained to discover geometric relationships implicitly inside a unified feature space. I wanted to test a different premise: could a network become more stable under large rotation if image evidence were organized according to geometric type before attention?

The resulting framework is inspired by the grade structure of Clifford Algebra. Scalar channels represent magnitude and structure, vector channels preserve direction, and bivector-derived rotor channels encode local rotational tendency. These streams remain separated through early projection and attention, then are fused during coarse-to-fine geometric decoding.

The project covers the complete research cycle: mathematical framing, architecture design, implementation, training-system debugging, benchmark construction, ablation analysis, qualitative visualization, and preparation of a journal manuscript with Deokwoo Lee.

Problem
Dense geometric alignment under severe in-plane rotation and ambiguous visual evidence.
Core idea
Represent scalar, directional, and rotational cues as separate grade-aware streams.
Output
A dense correspondence representation and a global rotation-translation transform.
Research focus
Rotation robustness, stable failure behavior, and parameter-efficient refinement.
02

The Starting Question

From distance metrics to a different mathematical language

The research began with a simple observation. Many learning objectives measure error through familiar Euclidean or L1-style distances. I wondered whether changing only the loss was too narrow, and whether a different mathematical system could shape the representation itself.

While surveying mathematical frameworks, I encountered Clifford Algebra: a system that places scalars, vectors, bivectors, and higher-grade quantities inside one multivector representation. Its ability to express oriented planes and rotations suggested a natural connection to image matching under large in-plane rotation.

That led to the central research hypothesis: rather than asking one latent feature tensor to learn every geometric property implicitly, explicitly separate magnitude, direction, and rotational interaction, then design attention and refinement around those roles.

03

Research Evolution

How the architecture changed through failure

The final system was not designed in one pass. Each redesign narrowed the research question, exposed a new failure mode, and moved the implementation closer to a testable geometric hypothesis.

01

Initial End-to-End Prototype

Apply the mathematical idea across the whole pipeline

The first architecture attempted to connect custom geometric preprocessing, Clifford-inspired features, and end-to-end transformation prediction immediately. It was conceptually broad, but too many components changed at once, making optimization failures difficult to isolate.

Lesson: a mathematical concept needs explicit interfaces and testable stages.
02

v1 Multi-Stream Redesign

Separate geometric roles before learning to fuse them

The next design organized image evidence into scalar, vector, and bivector-related paths and introduced multi-scale processing. This made the representation more interpretable, but the model still required a clearer matching objective and a better way to propagate transforms across resolutions.

Lesson: separation is useful only when the decoder respects the same geometry.
03

Matching-Focused Rebuild

Reframe the system around correspondence and transform estimation

I rebuilt the model around a coarse-to-fine geometric transformer. Source features were pre-warped by the current estimate, each level predicted a residual update, and forward/backward transform conventions were made explicit for sampling and supervision.

Lesson: the transformation convention is part of the architecture, not an implementation detail.
04

V4 Diagnostic Stage

Identify under-rotation, constant prediction, and unstable refinement

Large-rotation experiments showed that the model could learn a geometric trend while still collapsing toward biased predictions. Earlier refinement logic either changed the estimate too aggressively or stayed near identity. I added curriculum training, deep transform supervision, and checkpoint guards that reject identity-like or constant-prediction solutions.

Lesson: a lower training loss does not guarantee pair-dependent geometric reasoning.
05

Final Five-Stage Framework

Bounded direct residual refinement plus inference-time energy optimization

The final architecture replaced unstable iterative refinement with a zero-initialized direct residual head and added a parameter-free geometric optimizer. The paper, implementation, losses, diagrams, and evaluation protocol were then synchronized around the same five-stage pipeline.

Outcome: a compact, staged framework with clearer behavior and measurable recovery cases.
04

Final Architecture

Five stages from raw geometry to refined alignment

P1

Geometric Feature Extraction

Gaussian pyramid with scalar, vector, and wedge-product rotation cues.

P2

Clifford Embedding

Grade-decomposed scalar, vector, and rotor streams.

P3

Geometric Transformer

Rotor-scale attention and coarse-to-fine transform composition.

P4

Direct Residual Refiner

Bounded correction from warped feature discrepancy.

P5

Energy Optimization

Gate-weighted inference-time geometric consistency.

P1

Raw geometric evidence

Multi-scale feature extraction

  • Scalar cues: CLAHE texture, structure-tensor anisotropy, edge magnitude, and an edge-distance potential field.
  • Vector cues: normalized Sobel gradient and structure-aware texture-flow direction.
  • Bivector candidate: signed 2D wedge product between gradient and texture flow.
  • Multi-scale context: the same cues are extracted over a Gaussian pyramid.
P2

Grade-aware representation

Clifford multivector embedding

  • Scalar stream: smooth Softplus projection, learnable scaling, and global descriptor gating.
  • Vector stream: bias-free projection preserves directional linearity.
  • Rotor stream: normalized cosine, sine, and magnitude represent direction and scale tendency.
  • Explicit GP branch: dot and wedge cues enter through a zero-initialized residual path.
P3

Initial geometric estimator

Multivector-aware transformer

  • Grouped tokenization: scalar, vector, and rotor channels gain local context without immediate mixing.
  • Independent Q/K/V: each grade receives its own linear projection.
  • Rotor-scale attention: rotation similarity and log-scale distance bias token interaction.
  • Coarse-to-fine decoder: pre-warp, cross-attend, predict a dense residual rotor field, pool by confidence, and compose transforms.
  • Memory control: chunked attention and local windows keep high-resolution processing feasible.
P4

Learned global correction

Direct residual geometric refiner

  • Input descriptor: pooled target features, warped source features, signed difference, absolute difference, and seed-transform parameters.
  • Bounded prediction: residual rotation is limited to 35 degrees and normalized translation to 0.25.
  • Stable initialization: the final layer is zero-initialized, so refinement starts as an identity residual.
  • Composition: the residual is composed with the backward sampling transform rather than replacing it.
P5

Parameter-free final adjustment

Geometric energy optimization

  • Initialization: begins from the refined backward affine transformation.
  • Objective: minimizes gate-weighted scalar, vector, and rotor consistency energies.
  • Constraint: only a bounded similarity-style residual is optimized, preserving the learned seed.
  • Trade-off: no new trainable parameters, but additional inference-time computation.
05

Architecture Decisions

What was deliberately preserved

Separate before fusion

Geometric types are projected and tokenized independently so that early layers do not erase their roles.

Predict residual geometry

Each stage corrects an existing transform instead of relearning alignment from zero.

Preserve a valid seed

Zero initialization and bounded updates prevent refinement from destroying a useful initial estimate.

Make conventions explicit

Backward transforms are used for sampling; forward transforms are produced by inversion for supervision and evaluation.

Training and Evaluation

A protocol centered on severe rotation

Training

Curriculum to ±90°

Rotation difficulty increases by stage, preventing the model from facing the full angular range before learning basic geometric behavior.

  • Input resolution: 256 x 256
  • Batch size: 1 image pair
  • Gradient accumulation: 16
  • Scale jitter and image augmentation
Benchmarks

Multiple scene distributions

The evaluation separates overall accuracy from hard-rotation behavior and ambiguous visual conditions.

  • COCO rotation benchmark
  • Fixed angles from 0° to 90°
  • Low-gradient / ill-posed subset
  • Repetitive-pattern subset
  • HPatches and MegaDepth transfer
Metrics

Geometry, not only matching score

Each prediction is evaluated through transform-level and image-domain alignment measures.

  • Mean Average Corner Error
  • Alignment Error
  • Angle Error
  • Angular standard deviation
  • Mean rank and recovery analysis

Experimental Results

Strengths, recovery behavior, and remaining gaps

The final model did not outperform SIFT+RANSAC or DKM in overall alignment accuracy. Its main result is different: compared with several learned matchers, it showed a lower-variance and more gradual degradation pattern when in-plane rotation became severe. The portfolio page presents both sides of that result rather than treating one favorable subset as the whole conclusion.

Engineering and Research Challenges

Problems that forced the final redesign

1Turning algebra into a learnable representation
Challenge

Calling a tensor a multivector does not preserve geometric meaning. A direct fusion path allowed appearance features to dominate the intended structure.

Response

I defined explicit scalar, vector, and bivector-derived cues, preserved their separation through projection and tokenization, and introduced the geometric-product branch only as a zero-initialized residual.

2Refinement either overshot or did nothing
Challenge

Earlier iterative and safety-gated refiners could destroy a good P3 estimate, while conservative settings made P4 nearly identical to P3.

Response

I replaced the mechanism with a direct residual head trained by the final consistency objective. Zero initialization and bounded tanh outputs preserve the seed while allowing gradients immediately.

3Backward and forward transform confusion
Challenge

PyTorch warping expects an output-to-input sampling transform, while evaluation and paper equations often describe the opposite direction.

Response

The final code explicitly stores B-to-A for sampling, derives A-to-B by inversion at the loss boundary, and uses the same align-corners convention throughout training and inference.

4High-resolution attention and rotation collapse
Challenge

Full attention became expensive at fine resolutions, while training could still converge to image-independent or under-rotated predictions.

Response

I combined chunked attention, local-window fine-level matching, staged rotation curriculum, multi-level angle supervision, and checkpoint guards based on prediction variance and target correlation.

10

Reflection

What this project changed in how I conduct research

What I learned
  • A strong mathematical analogy becomes useful only after every tensor, operation, and loss has a concrete geometric role.
  • Architecture diagrams, code conventions, and equations must be revised together; otherwise a plausible paper can describe a different system from the implementation.
  • Failure distributions can reveal value that a single average metric hides, but they must be presented without obscuring stronger baselines.
  • Refinement should be designed to preserve a good estimate first and improve it second.
Current limitations
  • The framework remains less precise than SIFT+RANSAC and DKM on the overall synthetic-rotation benchmark.
  • Small and moderate rotations remain weaker than several established methods.
  • P5 produces strong corrections in favorable cases, but does not consistently beat P4 on every hard subset.
  • The model is Clifford-inspired and grade-structured; it is not presented as a fully Clifford-equivariant network.
Next direction

From global transform recovery to richer correspondence geometry

The next step is to improve local correspondence precision while preserving rotation robustness: stronger pair-dependent descriptors, uncertainty-aware residual routing, better scale and viewpoint modeling, and evaluation on non-synthetic geometric transformations.

A Clifford-Structured Geometric Transformer for Rotation-Aware Geometric Alignment

I designed a five-stage geometric alignment framework that organizes image evidence into scalar, vector, and rotor-related streams, then estimates and refines a global image-pair transform under severe in-plane rotation. Pixel-wise fields are used internally for residual geometry and confidence, while the principal reported output is the final transformation between the two images.

01

Overview

What this research investigates

Image-pair alignment systems often encode appearance and geometry together inside a unified latent feature space. I investigated a different premise: could global transform estimation become more stable under large in-plane rotation if magnitude, direction, and rotation-related evidence were organized by geometric role before attention?

The resulting framework is inspired by the grade structure of Clifford Algebra. Scalar cues describe texture and structural energy, vector cues preserve local direction, and a normalized bivector candidate is combined with the vector cues to infer a rotor-related representation. These streams remain separated through early projection and encoder processing, then are mixed in the coarse-to-fine decoder to estimate the image-pair transformation.

Problem
Global geometric alignment under severe in-plane rotation and ambiguous visual evidence.
Core idea
Represent scalar, directional, and rotation-related cues as separate grade-aware streams.
Internal prediction
A pixel-wise residual transform field, confidence gates, and refined geometric features.
Final output
A refined global backward affine transform for sampling and its inverse for evaluation.
02

Hypothesis

From implicit geometry to a grade-structured representation

Core question

Could the representation carry geometric roles more explicitly?

The work started from a question about Euclidean distance-based objectives in image matching, then moved toward a broader design question: instead of only changing the loss, could the feature representation itself be reorganized around geometric structure?

01

Implicit geometry

Standard learned features often encode appearance, direction, scale, and rotation inside one unified tensor, leaving their geometric relationships to be learned indirectly.

02

Grade-aware split

Clifford Algebra suggested a way to separate magnitude, direction, and rotation-related evidence as distinct but connected quantities.

03

Rotor-related cue

Scalar, vector, and bivector-candidate cues are extracted explicitly, then mapped into a learned representation for global transform estimation.

Clifford Algebra is used here as architectural inspiration, not as a claim of full Clifford equivariance. The hypothesis is that clearer geometric roles can make severe-rotation alignment easier to reason about inside the model.

03

Pipeline

Five phases from raw geometry to refined alignment

1Geometric Feature Extraction

Gaussian pyramid with scalar, vector, and normalized bivector-candidate cues.

2Clifford Embedding

Grade-decomposed scalar, vector, and learned rotor-related streams.

3Geometric Transformer

Rotor-scale-aware encoding and coarse-to-fine global transform composition.

4Direct Residual Refiner

Bounded correction from warped feature discrepancy.

5Energy Optimization

Gate-weighted inference-time geometric consistency.

1

Raw geometric evidence

Multi-scale feature extraction

  • Scalar cues: CLAHE texture, structure-tensor anisotropy, edge magnitude, and an edge-distance potential field.
  • Vector cues: normalized Sobel gradient and structure-aware texture-flow direction.
  • Bivector candidate: signed 2D wedge product between gradient and texture flow.
2

Grade-aware representation

Clifford multivector embedding

  • Scalar stream: smooth Softplus projection, learnable scaling, and global descriptor gating.
  • Vector stream: bias-free projection preserves directional linearity.
  • Rotor-related stream: normalized cosine, sine, and magnitude are inferred from vector cues and the bivector candidate.
3

Initial geometric estimator

Multivector-aware transformer

  • Grouped tokenization: scalar, vector, and rotor channels gain local context without immediate mixing.
  • Rotor-scale attention: rotation similarity and log-scale distance bias token interaction.
  • Coarse-to-fine decoder: pre-warp, cross-attend, predict a pixel-wise residual transform field, pool it by confidence, and compose the global update.
4

Learned global correction

Direct residual geometric refiner

  • Input descriptor: pooled target features, warped source features, signed difference, absolute difference, and seed-transform parameters.
  • Bounded prediction: residual rotation is limited to 35 degrees and normalized translation to 0.25.
5

Inference-only final adjustment

Geometric energy optimization

  • Objective: minimizes gate-weighted scalar, vector, and rotor consistency energies.
  • Trade-off: no new trainable parameters, but additional inference-time computation.

Research Challenges

Implementation problems resolved during development

1Refinement either overshot or did nothing
Challenge

Earlier iterative and safety-gated refiners could destroy a good P3 estimate, while conservative settings made P4 nearly identical to P3.

Response

I replaced the mechanism with a direct residual head trained by the final consistency objective. Zero initialization and bounded tanh outputs preserve the seed while allowing gradients immediately.

2Backward and forward transform confusion
Challenge

PyTorch warping expects an output-to-input sampling transform, while evaluation and paper equations often describe the opposite direction.

Response

The final code explicitly stores B-to-A for sampling, derives A-to-B by inversion at the loss boundary, and uses the same align-corners convention throughout training and inference.

3High-resolution attention and rotation collapse
Challenge

Full attention became expensive at fine resolutions, while training could still converge to image-independent or under-rotated predictions.

Response

I used resolution-specific computation, retaining local-window cross-attention on pre-aligned fine-level features while omitting the most expensive full-resolution encoder attention where necessary. Training pairs followed a staged rotation curriculum.

08

Reflection

What this project changed in how I conduct research

What This Study Revealed
  • Refinement should first preserve a reliable estimate and only then attempt to improve it.
  • Conducting extensive ablation studies strengthened my ability to identify the source of performance limitations and evaluate targeted solutions.
  • Because full training runs were time-consuming, incorrect design choices were sometimes discovered too late. This highlighted the importance of using smoke tests and small-scale validation experiments to verify ideas before committing to long training cycles.
Limitations and Next Steps
  • Fine local alignment and global transform precision both require further improvement.
  • P5 can strongly correct selected cases but should be activated selectively through confidence-aware criteria or adaptive stopping.
  • The model is Clifford-inspired and grade-structured; it is not presented as a fully Clifford-equivariant network.

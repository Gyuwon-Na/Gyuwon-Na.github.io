Game Development

Stand-By

Mar 2024 - Jun 2024 GAM 150 Project
C++ Raylib Cafe Simulation Team Project
Stand-By Gameplay Capture

Overview

Stand-By is a cafe management game created by Team Half & Half. The player runs a cafe as both the manager and the only employee, serving customers while trying to earn enough money to pay late rent.

The core interaction loop centers on making drinks under pressure. Players pour sodas, prepare blends with ingredients, shake the shaker, chill the cup, and deliver completed drinks to customers before the cafe closes.

Project Focus

  • Built a cafe workflow around taking orders, choosing drink types, preparing recipes, and serving customers.
  • Used separate game states for splash, main menu, gameplay, daily script, success ending, and fail ending.
  • Modeled drink-making objects such as cups, soda types, ingredients, recipe book, shaker, shaker gauge, cup chilling, and ice.
  • Supported customer seats, a closing-time clock, drink icons, and background elements as part of the play screen.

My Role

I worked as the tech lead for Stand-By. According to the team post-mortem, my responsibility was overall code management, working closely with QA as bugs were found and discussed during implementation.

Technical Highlights

Object Interaction

Used the mouse as the central interaction object for selecting tools, adding ingredients, shaking, chilling, and serving.

Drink Workflow

Structured soda and blend preparation through tool selection, recipe ingredients, shaker stages, and cup chilling.

Customer System

Customer behavior used random orders, visible and invisible timers, seat ranges, and randomized customer textures.

Game Systems

  • Customer orders could include 6 soda types and 11 blend types, with blend recipes unlocking gradually by in-game day.
  • Customer timing used an invisible timer before appearing and a visible timer while waiting for service.
  • The customer collision range was tied to each seat so the interaction stayed aligned with where customers were placed.
  • The cafe closes at 18:00, and the game can reach success or fail endings depending on money and day progression.

Challenges & Solutions

1 Customer System Complexity
Challenge

The post-mortem notes that the customer system was not as mature as the team wanted, and potential improvements became clear late in the schedule.

Solution

The final system still supported random orders, timed customer behavior, seat-based interaction ranges, and randomized customer textures.

2 Drink-Making Collision
Challenge

Many actions depended on collisions between the mouse, toolbar objects, ingredients, cups, shakers, and customer seats.

Solution

The team separated interaction rules by stage, including toolbar selection, ingredient-to-shaker checks, shaker movement, and cup chilling distance checks.

3 Planning and Code Communication
Challenge

The team tried a development priority spreadsheet but stopped using it properly later in the semester, and the post-mortem also mentions a lack of reading each other's code.

Solution

The team kept documentation, set priorities through communication, and generally met deadlines while implementing most desired features.

Reflection

Stand-By showed how quickly a management game becomes a coordination problem between UI, input, object interaction, customer behavior, recipes, and schedule pressure. The project also made clear that technical leadership is not only about writing code; it is also about keeping the team aligned around priorities, documentation, bug reports, and shared understanding of the codebase.

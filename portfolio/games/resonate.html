Game Development

Resonate

Sep 2023 - Dec 2023 Technical Lead GAM100 Project
C++ Raylib
Resonate Demo Video

Overview

Resonate is a single-player top-view boss rush game built by Team Star-Link for GAM 100. I worked as the Technical Lead and Game Programmer, focusing on the player systems, boss pattern logic, collision behavior, restart flow, and the overall stability of the C++/raylib implementation.

The original plan was to make combat feel less repetitive by combining boss attacks with fast, pattern-based challenges. By the final milestone, the project became a compact RPG-style boss raid where the player reads basic and special attack patterns, survives long enough to understand them, and finds openings to defeat the boss.

Key Achievements

  • Complete Game Loop: Built my first complete game within a short development cycle, including menu, play, and reset states.
  • Traceable Input Flow: Separated input capture from gameplay response to make behavior easier to trace and debug.
  • Technical Coordination: Coordinated technical decisions as the team adapted the design, rewrote unstable systems, and reduced scope before the final milestone.

Project Focus

  • Built a readable boss fight around repeated attempts, pattern recognition, and high-pressure attacks.
  • Connected keyboard movement, mouse attacks, healing, health state, boss behavior, and end conditions into one playable loop.
  • Used playtesting to tune pacing, reduce downtime, and make special patterns feel understandable instead of arbitrary.
  • Balanced ambitious ideas against the deadline by cutting features that did not strengthen the final experience.

Technical Highlights

Input & Control

Implemented responsive player input with clear separation between input capture and gameplay response.

Boss Patterns

Programmed real-time attack patterns with distinct behavior so the boss felt threatening and learnable.

Feedback Loop

Linked animation, audio, health, and state changes so player actions and boss pressure were easier to read.

Challenges & Solutions

1 Resolving Unstable Collision Behavior
Challenge

During development, collision bugs could trap the player on or under the boss, breaking the fight and making repeated attempts frustrating.

Solution

I separated movement from collision correction and added explicit position recovery when the player overlapped the boss. Repeated edge-case testing helped prevent the player from becoming trapped and made close-range interactions more consistent.

2 Rewriting Unstable Code
Challenge

As more patterns and interactions were added, the team found that the code was not organized well enough to keep debugging safely.

Solution

We made the difficult decision to rewrite large parts of the project. That reset cost time, but it gave us a cleaner structure for the final build.

3 Scope and Deadline Pressure
Challenge

The initial design included several exciting ideas, but not every idea could be finished to a reliable standard within the milestone schedule.

Solution

We focused on the strongest parts of the game: boss presence, attack patterns, visual animation, and a complete playable loop.

Reflection

Resonate was my first serious lesson in how quickly a game project becomes a systems problem. The final result was not just about implementing movement or attacks; it was about keeping many small behaviors reliable while art, audio, design, and code were all changing at the same time.

The most important lesson was that good development requires tradeoffs. We had to discard some interesting ideas, rewrite code when the structure stopped supporting us, and use playtesting to decide what actually made the fight better. By the end, I had a much stronger understanding of technical ownership, debugging discipline, and why planning matters even for a small team project.

Overview

The 1/2-Scale Autonomous Vehicle project was developed for the Student Creative Mobility Competition 2025, where our team successfully advanced to the finals in the Unmanned Mobility Division. The core objective was to navigate complex, dynamic obstacle courses by executing critical missions such as traffic light compliance, pedestrian emergency braking, obstacle avoidance, and target-specific autonomous delivery.

As the lead perception engineer, I spearheaded the vision-based perception pipeline and system architecture. My primary contributions included engineering illumination-robust algorithms for lane and traffic light detection, developing a custom auto-labeling tool to streamline YOLO model training, and refactoring the mission management architecture into a decoupled Dictionary structure for highly scalable deployment.

Challenges & Solutions

1 Overcoming Severe Backlight in Traffic Light Recognition
Challenge

Because the competition track was a closed-loop, the vehicle faced severe sun glare (backlight) when driving back from the opposite direction. This caused the vision sensor to fail in detecting traffic light color changes, leading to critical departure delays at intersections.

Solution

Inspired by smartphone camera image processing techniques, I built a dynamic image preprocessing pipeline with illumination robustness.

  • Applied CLAHE (Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization) to maximize local contrast and effectively restore colors obscured by backlight.
  • Integrated Gamma Correction and real-time saturation/contrast adjustment logic to ensure stable and highly accurate traffic light recognition regardless of external lighting conditions.
2 Robust Lane Detection Against Drastic Lighting Shifts
Challenge

The initial lane detection algorithm suffered a sharp drop in recognition rates in dark environments. Manually readjusting the HSL color thresholds for every lighting change was highly inefficient and posed a massive risk for adapting to unpredictable weather and lighting conditions on the competition day.

Solution

Moved away from relying on absolute color values and completely revamped the system with an L-Channel (Lightness) contrast-based detection algorithm.

  • Extracted only the Lightness data to perform morphological operations that maximize the relative contrast difference between the lane lines and the road surface.
  • Consequently, the system achieved robust lane tracking capabilities that autonomously adapt to ambient brightness changes without any manual parameter intervention.
3 Automated Data Labeling Pipeline for Custom Delivery Signs
Challenge

When training the YOLO model, there was no pre-existing auto-labeling feature for the competition-specific delivery target markers (e.g., A1, B1). This created a severe bottleneck, requiring the team to perform tedious manual labeling on thousands of images.

Solution

To eliminate the inefficiency of manual labor, I developed a Custom Auto-Labeling Tool that fuses various computer vision techniques to fully automate the training data pipeline.

  • Color-filtered Shape Detection: Initially filtered for square contours exhibiting specific blue hues.
  • Optical Character Recognition (OCR): Performed secondary verification to confirm the presence of text within the extracted Region of Interest (ROI).
  • By generating bounding box coordinates only when both conditions were strictly met, the pipeline minimized false positives and achieved approximately 98% labeling accuracy.
4 Algorithm Evolution & Trade-off Analysis
Challenge (Discarded Approaches)

To achieve optimal performance, I implemented and evaluated multiple algorithms. However, due to physical constraints and computational inefficiencies, the following approaches were ultimately discarded after a thorough trade-off analysis.

  • Lane Detection (HSL + Otsu's Binarization):
    Initially, I implemented a pipeline combining HSL color space conversion, morphological closing, and Otsu's binarization. However, this approach proved highly vulnerable in outdoor environments where rapid illumination changes caused the static color thresholds to collapse completely.
  • Obstacle Avoidance 1 (Grid-based Planning):
    I attempted to generate paths by excluding grid cells occupied by bounding boxes. However, this led to a geometric misalignment, as the vehicle's center often lay on the grid lines rather than inside the cells. Additionally, the excessive number of unnecessarily occupied buffer cells made advanced, smooth trajectory generation impossible.
  • Obstacle Avoidance 2 (Tangent-based Circular Avoidance):
    I generated avoidance trajectories by creating a circumscribed circle around the bounding box and following its tangent lines. While computationally fast, the calculated steering angles were overly aggressive, preventing fine steering control and causing the vehicle to deviate significantly from the track.
Solution (Final Architecture)

After evaluating these discarded approaches, I shifted the obstacle-avoidance module toward a more conservative lane-aware trajectory selection strategy. Instead of producing aggressive geometric detours from a single bounding-box estimate, the final architecture prioritized stable steering commands, bounded lateral motion, and predictable recovery back to the reference lane. This made the behavior easier to tune during field tests and reduced the risk of sudden control changes caused by noisy obstacle detections.

Reflection & Future Work

This project provided profound hands-on experience in vision-based perception and real-world hardware integration. By analyzing the system's limitations and the iterative challenges faced during field testing, I have identified key areas for my future growth as a computer vision and robotics engineer.

  • Deepening Interest in Computational Imaging: Developing robust lane and traffic light detection pipelines sparked a profound interest in how light interacts with physical environments. Moving forward, I aspire to delve deeper into Inverse Rendering and Computational Imaging?îresearching how to mathematically process light to extract precise physical properties and desired information from observed images.
  • Leveraging Open-Source for Algorithmic Breakthroughs: When encountering algorithmic roadblocks, I actively researched and analyzed various open-source implementations on GitHub. By dissecting complex external codebases and seamlessly integrating them into our custom system architecture, I significantly enhanced my code-reading proficiency and my ability to rapidly adapt and apply new technologies to solve practical engineering problems.
  • Importance of System Integration & Diagnostics: During field testing, I spent significant time debugging what I assumed were algorithmic failures, only to discover that the control node had silently lost connection with the physical hardware. This experience highlighted the absolute necessity of implementing robust heartbeat mechanisms and continuous hardware diagnostics (Health Monitoring) to ensure system reliability.
  • Optimizing Real-Time Perception Algorithms: During high-speed delivery missions, the sign recognition system occasionally failed, likely due to motion blur or computational overload. To resolve this in future projects, I plan to explore model optimization techniques to guarantee real-time, lightweight inference even under heavy system constraints.
  • Systematic Approach to Control System Tuning: Mitigating lateral oscillations during lane tracking required exhaustive manual debugging to find the optimal PID gains. This tedious fine-tuning process made me realize the necessity of systematic tuning methods, motivating me to study advanced control strategies to mathematically ensure stability rather than relying on empirical trials.
